Knee Pain Navigator

Five quick questions, built only on published rules doctors use. It tells you how soon to get your knee checked and where to start reading. It cannot diagnose you or rule anything out.

1. Fever, or a knee that is hot, red and swollen?
2. Did the pain start with an injury (fall, twist, blow) in the last week?

Tick any that apply:

3. Does the knee lock — get stuck so you can't straighten it?
4. Pain at night or at rest, or weight loss you can't explain?
5. Your knee pain day to day

Where these questions come from

  • Question 1 — a hot, swollen joint with fever can be septic arthritis (a joint infection). Patient.info, Knee assessment.
  • Question 2 — the Ottawa Knee Rules. In an acute knee injury, an X-ray is needed if any one of: age 55 or older, tenderness at the head of the fibula, isolated tenderness of the kneecap, inability to flex to 90°, or inability to bear weight both immediately and when examined (four steps). The rule was 100% sensitive for knee fractures in validation (1,096 adults). Stiell IG et al. Ann Emerg Med 1995 (doi); JAMA 1996 (PubMed 8594242). The rule was designed for a doctor's examination — your own answers can only tell you to get checked, not that nothing is broken.
  • Question 3–4 — locking suggests a loose body or meniscal injury; night or rest pain suggests a bone tumour must be excluded. Patient.info.
  • Question 5 — NICE guideline NG226 (2022), rec. 1.1.1: osteoarthritis is diagnosed clinically, without imaging, in people aged 45 or over with activity-related joint pain and no morning stiffness or stiffness lasting no longer than 30 minutes. Rec. 1.2.2: the core treatments are therapeutic exercise and weight management (if appropriate). NICE NG226.
